# W10. E4 and prior work: what was checked, and what is new or not (2026-10-21)

**Status.** Written at the workplace on 2026-10-21. **Only the papers below were checked,** through their arXiv pages
(abstract or full text, read with a summarising fetch). Anything not listed, or marked "not checked", was not
verified.

## Checked

| work | what it optimises | form along one parameter | how the minimum is found | evaluations per parameter | noise and bias | nonlinear losses (for example Brier / squared error, classification) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Rotosolve** (Ostaszewski, Grant, Benedetti; arXiv 1905.09692; Quantum, 2021) | an expectation value ⟨M⟩ ("the energy"); VQE and a state-overlap task | A sin(θ + B) + C, for gates exp(−iθH/2) with H Hermitian and unitary | closed form (arctan2) | 3 | shot noise discussed (1,000 measurements, "robust"); convergence analysed only for infinite shots; **no bias analysis** | **not addressed** |
| **Sequential minimal optimisation** ("NFT"; Nakanishi, Fujii, Todo; arXiv 1903.12166; Phys. Rev. Research 2, 043158, 2020) | a weighted sum of expectation values | a sine curve; for a parameter shared S times, a Fourier series up to S harmonics | the constants fitted, then the minimum of the fitted curve (DFT on a grid for several harmonics; closed form for one) | 2 new (+1 kept); 2S for shared parameters | notes that statistical error accumulates and re-estimates the cost periodically; **no bias analysis** | **not covered** (relies on linearity in the expectation values) |
| **ExcitationSolve** (Jäger, Kaldenbach, Haas, Schultheis; arXiv 2409.05939; journal version DOI 10.1038/s42005-025-02375-9) | VQE energy with excitation generators (G³ = G) | **a second-order Fourier series** (frequencies up to 2θ; 5 coefficients) | **the zeros of the derivative through a companion matrix of a degree-4 polynomial**; the energy evaluated at each real root, and the smallest taken | 5 (4 new) | not checked | not addressed (energies only, as far as checked) |

**Found but not read** (not checked): Wierichs et al., "General parameter-shift rules for quantum gradients"
(arXiv 2107.12390; Quantum, 2022), on reconstructing univariate trigonometric functions. Its relation to E4 is not
verified here.

**Searched without a clear answer** (not checked): whether a per-parameter exact minimisation of a squared-error or
Brier classification loss, where the second harmonic comes from the loss rather than from the generator, has been
published for quantum classifiers. One search returned no paper that clearly does this. That is not evidence that
none exists.

## Same and different, as far as checked

| E4 element | status against the checked work |
|---|---|
| each parameter is one rotation, so the output is exactly a + b cos u + c sin u along it | **the same principle** as Rotosolve and NFT |
| a second-order trigonometric loss along u, minimised exactly through a quartic (companion matrix or roots in tan(u/2)) | **the same technique** as ExcitationSolve (degree-4 polynomial; evaluate the real roots, take the smallest). In ExcitationSolve the second harmonic comes from the generator; in E4 it comes from the Brier loss, squared per data point |
| the Brier loss of a classifier (quadratic in each point's ⟨Z⟩), summed over training points | **not found** in the checked papers, which optimise linear functions of expectation values. Prior work elsewhere: not checked |
| unbiased coefficients under trajectory noise, from the product of two independent halves | **not found** in the checked papers (they discuss shot noise but not bias). The statistical idea itself (a product of independent estimates is unbiased) is standard |
| compared against an SPSA-type optimiser (C) from the same start, at equal training cost, with a θ = 0 control | a design of the comparison, not of the method; not found in the checked papers |

## What can be said

- **Not new:**
  - the per-parameter exact sinusoid (Rotosolve, NFT);
  - the exact minimum of a second-order trigonometric function through a degree-4 polynomial (ExcitationSolve).
- **Possibly new** (not found in the three papers checked; a wider search was not made):
  - applying the exact per-parameter solve to a quadratic classification loss over data points;
  - the unbiased split-half coefficients under trajectory noise;
  - the equal-cost, same-start comparison against SPSA with a θ = 0 control.
- **Proposed wording for any write-up:** "E4 applies the per-parameter exact minimisation of Rotosolve / NFT, with
  ExcitationSolve's exact second-order solve, to the Brier loss of a quantum classifier, with unbiased coefficients
  under trajectory noise." No claim of novelty beyond "not found in the works cited".
