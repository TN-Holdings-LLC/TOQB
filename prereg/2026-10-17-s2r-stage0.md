# Pre-registration: TOQB S2R stage 0 -- many candidates chosen in a simulator, a few run on a stand-in device; does PSF-Zero keep the simulator's choice closer to the device's? (2026-10-17)

**Status: pre-registration.** Fixed by the commit that adds it and by its SHA-256, given to the owner before the run;
pushed by the owner before the run starts. The run is made once. Times in CEST. The design follows PSF-Zero's idea
memo (`docs/ideas/s2r-sim-to-real-2026-10-16.md`) and the owner's three decisions of 2026-10-17 (about 02:25 CEST):
today's classifier; a stand-in device with T1/T2, over-rotation and ZZ mismatches on ibm_marrakesh's calibration;
stage 0 with N = 10,000, k = 16, $192 and 20 seeds.

## 1. The question

A cheap simulator chooses which parameter vectors are sent to an expensive device. **Do PSF-Zero's circuits keep
"good in simulation" closer to "good on the device", so that the same device budget reaches a better model?** Stage 0
asks whether there is an effect at all; stages 1 (N = 100,000 on a rented GPU) and 2 (the real device) follow only if
there is.

## 2. What is run

**Code:** [`toqb/s2r.py`](../toqb/s2r.py) (the loop, the stand-in, the baseline; its module docstring is the full
description) and the scorer [`toqb/score_s2r.py`](../toqb/score_s2r.py); tests `tests/test_s2r.py`.
`CODE_SHA256SUMS` lists every TOQB file the run uses.

**The task** (TOQB's loop classifier, unchanged): n = 6, L = 8, data re-uploading on a ring; teacher seed 4, 64
training and 48 test points; loss (1 - y z) / 2. **Compilers:** Qiskit level 2's template and PSF-Zero's template
(a22 + c61, the same files and SHA-256 as the budget and reach runs), both compiling for FakeFez's Target.

**The simulator** (what the user has): TOQB's noise model on FakeFez, the exact noisy <Z_0> of each compiled circuit
(the fast engine, no shots), not billed.

**The stand-in device** (what the simulator does not know): ibm_marrakesh's calibration of 2026-10-13 11:37 CEST
(`toqb/data/ns_snapshot_ibm_marrakesh_2026-10-13.json`, the HW2 snapshot; the same coupling map as FakeFez), with

- every qubit's T1 and T2 multiplied by a factor uniform in [0.7, 1.3] (drawn once, seed 20261017);
- every sx and x over-rotated by 1% of its angle (a coherent RX error after the gate);
- a static ZZ interaction of 10 kHz between every pair of neighbouring qubits, an idle neighbour (in |0>) giving a Z
  rotation (checked against a global-Hamiltonian evolution in the tests);

scheduled ALAP with its own durations, evolved by Aer's density matrix, 400 shots per circuit. Billed at IBM's price
(0.036 s per circuit + 400 x (0.27 ms + the circuit's duration on FakeFez's Target), $96 per minute), as in TOQB.
**These magnitudes (30%, 1%, 10 kHz) were proposed by the authors and confirmed by the owner (2026-10-17, about 03:18 CEST)**; whether Heron's residual ZZ is as large as 10 kHz was not checked.

**S2R (per compiler and seed):** per loop, 10,000 candidates m + sigma_t eps (the mean is candidate 0); each
evaluated in the simulator on the loop's batch of 8 training points; the 16 with the lowest simulated loss run on the
stand-in on the same 8 points (128 circuits, billed); the mean moves to the log-weighted mean of the best 8 of the 16
by the stand-in's loss; sigma_{t+1} = 0.7 x sigma_t, sigma_0 = 1.0. Initial mean uniform in +-0.5. The loop
stops before the one that would pass $192. After every loop the mean is evaluated on the stand-in's 48 test points
(400 shots, not billed) and in the simulator.

**The device-only baseline (per compiler and seed):** TOQB's SPSA with run 1's settings (batch 8, a = 3.0, c = 0.2,
A = 2) on the stand-in, stopped before the job that would pass $192, evaluated on the stand-in's test set every 10
iterations and at the stop.

**Seeds:** 9001-9020 (no earlier run used them); the same draws (batches, eps, initial values, shot seeds) for both
compilers. **Plans:** [`s2r-stage0-2026-10-17/plan_s9001.json`](s2r-stage0-2026-10-17/) to `plan_s9020.json`, from
`make_plans.py`; `SHA256SUMS` has SHA-256 `ee1cf452a67124f7bbbccb8baacc056899fb61c24b5de54dc40e27717c38ca1e`.

**sigma_0 and its decay** were chosen from an exploratory pilot (section 7) on seed 901, which this run does not use.

**The machine and the command:** the owner's home PC (WSL), ten processes, with
[`run.sh`](s2r-stage0-2026-10-17/run.sh), which checks every hash and the versions, refuses an existing output folder,
and writes the results outside the repository. **Expected time:** about 7-8 hours (from the pilot: about 1.5-1.8 hours per S2R run with N = 10,000 and 7-8 minutes per SPSA run, on one core of the authors' cloud; 80 runs, ten at a time). About 0.6 GB of memory per process.

**Interruption rule:** a run that stops is started again into a new folder; the first complete run is scored.

## 3. Predictions

Per seed, d is a paired difference; the test is the one-sided sign-flip test of its mean (exact over the 2^20 sign
patterns), alpha = 0.01. A value between the thresholds is NOT DECIDED.

| ID | prediction | d per seed | confirmed if | refuted if |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| S1 | every one of the 80 runs valid (no error, no structural failure, no operation on a failed element) | - | all 80 | any not |
| S2 | **(primary)** with S2R, PSF-Zero's model reaches a higher stand-in test accuracy for $192 than Qiskit level 2's | PSF-Zero minus Qiskit, final stand-in test accuracy | mean d > 0 and p <= 0.01 | mean d <= 0 |
| S3 | with S2R, the simulator's ranking of the 16 candidates agrees better with the stand-in's for PSF-Zero | PSF-Zero minus Qiskit, the mean over loops of Spearman's rho between simulated and stand-in losses | as S2 | as S2 |
| S4 | for each compiler, S2R reaches a higher stand-in test accuracy for $192 than device-only SPSA | S2R minus SPSA, final stand-in test accuracy | both compilers as S2 | either mean d <= 0 |

**The authors' expectation, stated before the run** (not a criterion): S1 and S4 confirmed (in the pilot S2R reached 0.78-0.80 and SPSA 0.41-0.48 for the same money). S2 and S3 are open: in the pilot's chosen setting the two compilers ended close (0.80 and 0.78 on one seed), and with sigma_0 = 0.6 far apart (0.81 and 0.58), so one seed says little; NOT DECIDED is a likely outcome. PSF-Zero's circuits are about half as long on FakeFez (8.7 us against 18.1 us), but both compilers get six loops for $192 (the per-circuit charge and the shots dominate the price), so any difference has to come from noise, not from more loops.

## 4. Reported without prediction

- Per compiler and method: the mean final stand-in test accuracy, simulated and noiseless test accuracies, loops or
  iterations within the budget, dollars spent; the mean stand-in test accuracy after each loop.
- Wall time, simulator and stand-in seconds.

## 5. How it is read

- **Display:** the mean stand-in test accuracy each compiler's S2R model reaches for $192, against device-only SPSA.
- **Essential:** in how many of the 20 seeds PSF-Zero's S2R model is better, and whether the simulator's choice
  survives on the stand-in better for it (S3). One task, one stand-in, in simulation; 48 test points move in steps of
  1/48. **Stage 1 is proposed only if S2 or S3 is confirmed.**

## 6. What is not tested

The real device (stage 2); N = 100,000 (stage 1); other stand-in mismatches; other tasks.

## 7. The exploratory pilot (before this pre-registration; not scored)

Seed 901, N = 1,000 candidates per loop (a tenth of the run's), both compilers, the rest as in section 2. Three settings of sigma_0 and its decay were tried; the one with the higher mean final stand-in test accuracy over the two compilers was chosen (the rule was set before the third setting's result was read):

| 0.3, 0.8 | 0.31 0.32 0.31 0.28 0.50 0.38 0.43 | 0.28 0.30 0.48 0.52 0.54 0.56 0.58 | 0.51 |
| 0.6, 0.7 | 0.31 0.35 0.67 0.61 0.79 0.72 0.81 | 0.28 0.32 0.38 0.42 0.39 0.44 0.58 | 0.70 |
| **1.0, 0.7 (chosen)** | 0.31 0.51 0.65 0.65 0.76 0.81 0.80 | 0.28 0.40 0.73 0.73 0.81 0.71 0.78 | **0.79** |

Device-only SPSA on the same seed and stand-in: 0.48 (PSF-Zero, 50 iterations, $189) and 0.41 (Qiskit, 49 iterations, $190). Both compilers ran six S2R loops ($181 and $186). The pilot also found that 8,000 circuits simulated at once exhaust 8 GB of memory: the simulator now takes 400 at a time (the same computation in smaller batches).
