\subsection{How deep the observables read}\label{sec:utility-depth}
\texttt{results/utility-depth-2026-11-11}, ibm\_fez (ibm\_marrakesh placed no clear cone), prereg/utility\_depth\_2026-11-11.md.
The exact values were extended past the 11-10 depths by a light-cone tensor-network contraction (checked against the
stabilizer method on all 127 qubits at the Clifford points) and sealed before the run. One job of 137 circuits,
17 values of $\theta_h$ per observable:

$Z_{106}$ read at every depth run (4 to 8 steps), weight-10 at 5 and 6. The prediction that weight-10 would not read at
6 steps failed. Both readings need care. The exact $Z_{106}$ curve changes little with depth (the 4- and 8-step
curves differ by at most 0.11), and the MSE falls with depth because the device's tail at large $\theta_h$ shrinks: this
is reading a slowly changing answer, not a test of a fast-changing one. Weight-10 at 6 steps is a small signal (mean
square 0.0038, largest exact value 0.13): the device beats the all-zero guess (MSE 0.0005 against 0.0038) and puts its
largest values at the same $\theta_h$, but the READS line was not written for so small a signal; the verdict is given
as declared. Against 11-10 on the same device and circuits, $Z_{106}$ at 4 steps had MSE 0.0198 (0.0207) and weight-10
at 5 steps 0.0238 (0.0226). The mirror of each circuit fell with depth (0.48 to 0.32 for $Z_{106}$, 0.11 and 0.05 for
weight-10).
